# 01. Project Brief

## Name

**Swimphony**

## One-line pitch

A one-camera generative instrument that turns a goldfish's movement into music and ambient light.

## Product promise

A user points an ordinary camera at a simple aquarium, performs a short calibration, and hears and sees the fish's movement translated into a calm audiovisual performance. No wearable, tag, depth camera, or special aquarium equipment is required.

## Why the idea is strong

- The concept is visible immediately in a demo.
- The fish is both subject and performer.
- Computer vision, sound design, light design, and GPT-5.6 have distinct roles.
- The basic experience works for people without Philips Hue or the same aquarium.
- The project can grow into installations, aquarium exhibits, hospitality ambience, and other passive animal or environmental interfaces.

## Target user

Primary:

- A person with an aquarium who wants a playful, aesthetic home experience

Secondary:

- Artists and designers building small interactive installations
- Aquariums, cafés, hotels, galleries, and event spaces
- Educators demonstrating computer vision and generative media

## Core user journey

1. Select **Sample video** or **Camera**.
2. Mark the aquarium area.
3. Click the fish to calibrate its color.
4. Press **Start audio**.
5. The fish's movement controls sound and Virtual Light.
6. Enter a mood such as “quiet midnight aquarium.”
7. GPT-5.6 generates a new validated performance preset.
8. Optionally send the same ambient light state to Philips Hue.

## Emotional goal

The result should feel meditative, alive, and slightly magical, not like a sensor dashboard making random beeps. The interface should foreground the aquarium and the performance, with engineering details available only when needed.

## Ethical boundary

The system passively observes natural movement. It must never encourage tapping the glass, chasing the fish, flashing lights, feeding solely to create motion, or shining strong reactive light directly into the aquarium.
